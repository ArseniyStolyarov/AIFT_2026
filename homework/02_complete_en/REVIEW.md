## Consumer and welfare audit

Training starts from a valid expenditure function with positive targets,
`e = ubar p1^(1/4) p2^(3/4)`. The mock's harmonic direct utility is continuous
at the origin with value 0 and yields the stated indirect value. Its optimum
is positive and unique for positive prices/income.

The training and mock use distinct functions and numerical cases; neither is
the worked Seminar 3 pair. The training is deliberately scaffolded; the mock
requires recovery from a different value function.

All CV/EV values use positive monetary losses for a one-price increase at
fixed income. CS is explicitly the finite Marshallian area, with other prices
and income held fixed. Both examples have normal demands and satisfy
`EV < loss_CS < CV`; numerical integration independently checks the CS values.
No unqualified equality of the three welfare measures is asserted.

The stipend question only promises that the old basket remains affordable.
The key distinguishes weakly preserved welfare from exact Hicks compensation
and allows a strict gain from reoptimisation.

## Risk audit

- Wealth outcomes are positive and finite. Bernoulli indices are strictly
  increasing for certainty-equivalent and monetary-lottery arguments.
- Positive affine transformations preserve vNM rankings, CEs and defined
  Arrow–Pratt coefficients. Arbitrary increasing transformations need not.
- Both training lotteries have mean 68 and crossing CDFs. The stated coupling
  makes B a mean-preserving spread of A; SOSD is ungraded extra practice.
- Concavity implies a nonnegative risk premium. Strict concavity and
  nondegeneracy give strictness; one affine support interval does not imply
  global risk neutrality.
- Insurance premiums are paid in both states and indemnities only in the loss
  state. The required coverage bounds and positive-wealth domains are checked.
- Fair log insurance is full by Jensen; loaded insurance is partial or zero.
  The general wealth-dependent formula includes the zero-coverage boundary.
- The mock separately distinguishes a maximal full-insurance lump-sum fee
  from the optimum under a per-unit loaded coverage contract.
- The raffle's negative expected net payoff, independence and absence of
  nonmonetary benefits are explicit. Conditional Jensen supports its answer.
