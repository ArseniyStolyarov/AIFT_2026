\heading{Homework 2 · Instructor solutions}{Welfare and risk · Lectures 3 and 4}
\paragraph{Order and assessment.} Part I is the ordinary assessed training
set, out of 100: Training 1 is 30 points, Training 2 is 35 and Training 3 is 35.
It appears before Part II as requested. Part II is the 120-minute formative
mock, also out of 100: 50 true/false, 20 consumer welfare, 20 insurance and
10 short answers. Do not combine the two totals. Only Part I contributes to
the assessed homework score; the three homeworks remain equally weighted
within the course's 20\% component. No deadline has been supplied.

\paragraph{Scope of this preparation draft.} Lecture 3 covers consumer
duality and Slutsky. Lecture 4 is being planned to start with CV, EV and CS,
then introduce expected utility, risk aversion and insurance, following the
course syllabus's risk block. Its slides are not yet in this repository.
This draft uses that combined scope. FOSD is used in training and one mock
claim; the SOSD coupling is ungraded additional practice only.

\paragraph{Mock marking.} Each true/false claim is worth 5 points, normally
1 for the verdict and 4 for a correct justification; a bare label earns zero.
Accept equivalent valid counterexamples and give partial credit for useful
intermediate reasoning. The key is \textbf{T, F, F, F, T; T, F, T, F, F}.
The 120-minute paper is diagnostic: concise rigorous arguments suffice.

\paragraph{Suggested workload.} Part I has no time limit. A student who
already knows the tools should allow roughly two hours for the required
training and two for the mock, with extra time for review. The optional SOSD
exercise is not required or marked. This is a planning estimate, not a student
time restriction.
