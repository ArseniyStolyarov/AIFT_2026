\section*{Mock 1. True or false · 50 points}
Each claim is worth 5 points. For claims 1--5, utility is continuous on
$\R_+^2$, preferences are locally nonsatiated, $p\gg0$, $m>0$, and targets
are attainable and above $u(0)$. Do not assume convexity or differentiability
unless stated. At a regular point,
\[
 S_{ij}=\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial p_j}
       +x_j\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial m}.
\]
\task{1. Concavity without convex tastes}{}
For a fixed target, expenditure is concave in prices even if preferences
are not convex.
\task{2. Smooth in income is enough}{}
If $v_m(p,m)$ exists and is strictly positive, the utility-maximising bundle
at $(p,m)$ must be unique.
\task{3. Ordinary symmetry}{}
Whenever ordinary demand is single-valued and continuously differentiable,
\[
 \frac{\partial x_1}{\partial p_2}=\frac{\partial x_2}{\partial p_1}.
\]
\task{4. Strict tastes, strict curvature}{}
At a regular point with strictly convex preferences, the Slutsky matrix
satisfies $z^\top Sz<0$ for every nonzero $z$.
\task{5. One gradient, one basket}{}
If $e(\cdot,\bar u)$ is differentiable at positive prices p, the expenditure
problem for that target has a unique minimiser.
