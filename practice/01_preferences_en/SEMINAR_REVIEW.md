# Seminar review against Lecture 1

Reference: `lectures/01_preferences_en/lecture01.tex`, including the appendix.
The user reports that separability and quasilinearity were not reached live.
Those two topics are therefore unnumbered teaching sections, even though slides
for them are present in the deck. Their examples were also changed.

## Repetitions removed

- Exact Condorcet rankings (`condorcet`): removed from the seminar.
- Lexicographic square and its standard limit (`lexicographic`, `continuity`):
  replaced by a two-class VIP domain where representation *is* possible, even
  with unbounded secondary quality. The exercise explicitly contrasts the domains.
- `u=x_1` (`lns`): replaced by other models testing distinct LNS failure sets.
- Linear utility and its exponential (`strict-convex`, `transformations`): removed
  as a stand-alone exercise.
- Rescaled perfect complements (`complements`): replaced by maximum-of-two scores,
  capped additive utility, and a balance index. These have different contour
  geometry and satiation sets.
- The two-menu purchase example (`restaurants`, `warp-example`): replaced by a
  chain of pairwise ties inconsistent with the third full report. This tests
  transitivity of indifference rather than merely a direct strict reversal.
- The original food-index/aggregator (`separable-aggregator`): replaced by a
  logarithmic computer index and a multiplicative workspace aggregator.
- The square-root quasilinear spending example (`quasilinear-income`): replaced
  by a studio index with shifted roots and quadratic cost of attaining an index.

## New exercise constructions

1. Floor scores: a limit changes indifference into strict preference.
2. Maximum score: an upper contour is a union of half-spaces, not a rectangle.
3. Rain/sun robustness: an intersection of comparisons of two scenario scores.
   It practices incomplete orders, the same *concept* as the lecture's Pareto
   relation, with a new three-attribute model rather than reused bundles.
4. A penalty triggered by any positive amount: LNS with boundary discontinuity.
5. Bounded arctangent index: an unattained numerical bound is not satiation.
6. Absolute imbalance: the entire diagonal is best; upper contours are strips.
7. A gap in the commodity domain: strong monotonicity need not give LNS at an
   isolated point. The lecture's implication used the full orthant.
8. A discrete primary attribute changes the lexicographic representation issue.
9. Two capped goods: saturation occurs on a full quadrant, not at one/two peaks.
10. Pairwise full reports force two indifferences and one incompatible strict rank.
11. Studio upgrades: cheapest index attainment by a sum-of-squares inequality,
    followed by square completion and a low-income corner.

Definitions and theorems intentionally recur: students apply the lecture's tools.
The comparison concerns worked examples, domains and data, not avoiding course
concepts. The homework files were not changed in this revision.
