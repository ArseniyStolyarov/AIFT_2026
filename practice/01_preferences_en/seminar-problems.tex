\heading{Seminar 1}{Preferences and utility · Problem sheet}
Give a short justification for each answer. For a false claim, specify a counterexample. Unless stated otherwise, quantities are nonnegative and topology is relative to the stated domain. No Lagrangian methods are needed.

\task{1. Separability}{Opening questions}
Write a bundle as $(y,z)$, where $y=(t,c)$ denotes tea and coffee and $z$ is another good.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item What does it mean that preferences over $y$ are separable from $z$? State the condition using comparisons of $(y,z)$ and $(y',z)$ at different fixed values of $z$.
\item For $u(t,c,z)=\sqrt{tc}+\log(1+z)$, find an index $v(t,c)$ and an aggregator $U(v,z)$. Does separability require utility to be additively separable?
\item For $w(t,c,z)=t+zc$, compare $y=(2,0)$ and $y'=(0,2)$ at $z=1/2$ and at $z=2$. Is the food group separable from $z$?
\item Does a stable ranking within the food group imply a fixed food budget?
\end{enumerate}
\task{2. Quasilinearity}{Opening questions}
Let $a>0$, $b>0$. Consider $u=a+\sqrt b$, $v=e^{a+\sqrt b}$ and $w=a^2+b$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Define a utility function quasilinear in $a$. Which of these \emph{given functions} have that form?
\item Which rankings in this list admit a representation quasilinear in $a$? For $w$, compare $(a,b)=(1,4)$ and $(2,1)$, then add 1 to the $a$ coordinate of both bundles.
\item For $u$, how does an indifference curve change when its utility label rises by 1? Is ``no income effect on $b$'' valid at every income if $a\ge0$ is imposed?
\end{enumerate}
\task{3. A tolerance rule}{}
On $\R_+^2$, let $x\pref y$ iff $x_1+x_2\ge y_1+y_2-1$. Is the relation complete? Is it transitive? Can a utility function represent it?
\task{4. AND versus OR}{}
Compare $x\pref_A y\iff (x_1\ge y_1\ \text{and}\ x_2\ge y_2)$ with $x\pref_B y\iff (x_1\ge y_1\ \text{or}\ x_2\ge y_2)$. Which are complete? Which are transitive?
\task{5. Indifference from a primitive relation}{}
Define $\sim$ and $\succ$ using only $\pref$. If two bundles are incomparable, are they indifferent? For rational preferences, prove that $x\sim y$ and $y\sim z$ imply $x\sim z$.
\task{6. Rational people, cyclic majority}{}
Three voters rank $a\succ b\succ c$, $c\succ a\succ b$ and $b\succ c\succ a$. Compute the pairwise majority ranking. Is there a contradiction with individual rationality?
\task{7. A broken limit}{}
Lexicographic preferences on $[0,1]^2$ first compare good 1, then good 2. Are they continuous? Give a sequence of weak comparisons whose limiting comparison fails.
\task{8. Draw the right set}{}
For $u(x)=\min\{2x_1,x_2\}$, draw the indifference set through $(1,2)$ and its weak upper contour set. Are preferences convex? Strictly convex?
\task{9. Change the labels}{}
For $u=x_1+x_2$, which of $e^u$, $-e^{-u}$ and $\min\{u,1\}$ represent the same preferences? Can the same preferences have a convex and a nonconvex utility representation?
\task{10. A good that does not matter}{}
On $\R_+^2$, let $u=x_1$. Are preferences continuous, monotone, strongly monotone and locally nonsatiated? Here monotonicity means that increasing \emph{every} good strictly improves the bundle.
\task{11. A consumer who has enough}{}
For $u=-[(x_1-2)^2+(x_2-1)^2]$, are preferences convex? Strictly convex? Locally nonsatiated? Name the bundle that settles the last question.
\task{12. A purchase is not a full ranking}{}
A consumer selects $a$ from $\{a,b\}$ and $b$ from $\{a,b,c\}$. Can these be selections from one fixed rational ranking? Would your answer change if the records were the \emph{full} sets of best alternatives, respectively $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$?

\task{13. One larger problem: food and everything else}{}
A consumer has preferences represented by
\[
 u(a,t,c)=a+4(tc)^{1/4},\qquad (a,t,c)\in\R_+^3.
\]
All three prices are 1 and the budget is $a+t+c\le m$, where $m>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Write $u=U(v(t,c),a)$ using $v(t,c)=\sqrt{tc}$. Is the food group separable? Is utility quasilinear in $a$?
\item Draw a food indifference curve for $v=1$. For a fixed food index $v\ge0$, show that the cheapest way to obtain it has $t=c=v$ and costs $2v$. You may use $t+c\ge2\sqrt{tc}$; explain when equality holds.
\item Explain why any optimum exhausts the budget and buys the food index in a cheapest way. Reduce the problem to choosing $v\in[0,m/2]$.
\item Find the optimal $(a,t,c)$ for $m=1$, $m=2$ and $m=6$, then for general $m>0$. You may complete a square after setting $s=\sqrt v$.
\item Does separability fix total food spending? Does quasilinearity eliminate income effects at low incomes? Explain both answers using the solution.
\end{enumerate}
